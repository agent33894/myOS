People judge an experience largely by its peak and its end. The behavioral research rates this peak-end effect as strong, with a correlation of about .58 across 174 effect sizes. Most productivity software ends the day on the backlog. Close the day ends it on what you finished.

The first screen counts it: "You finished 5 things today. Take a moment with that before you set up tomorrow." On a lighter day it says, "A quieter day. Not every day is for finishing things."

Then comes "Where should the rest go?" Each task still open for today gets **Tomorrow**, **Later**, **Someday**, or **Keep**, one key each. Giving loose ends a place clears what the research calls attention residue, the leftover pull of an unfinished task.

Next, an optional line for today's journal page, appended under **Evening**: "A small win, a good conversation, a thought for tomorrow…" Leave it empty to skip.

It closes with a summary of what was settled and a finish line: "That's a wrap. The day is closed. Tomorrow can start fresh." Enter continues, Escape stops wherever you are, and the flow only ever starts when you start it.
