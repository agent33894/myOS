The household organizer in the walkthroughs found family life filed under `work/`. Every capture landed there, because 2.0 kept a folder structure the interface never mentioned.

3.0 asks once, during onboarding: "What's this space for? Pick the areas you'll use. They keep your files in tidy folders, and you can change them any time." The choices are **Work** ("Projects, meetings, and deadlines"), **Personal**, **Learning**, and **Creative**, with a default for new things. New notes and tasks take their project's area, then the default. There is no more hidden `work/`.

Every page now has an **Area** property. Choosing another area moves the file to that area's folder, keeps its name, takes a snapshot first, and offers Undo.

File names follow titles, too. About two seconds after you stop editing a title, the file is renamed to a readable `<slug>.md` in the same folder, with "-2" added if the name is taken. The rename is revision-checked, undoable, and never happens while you are still typing. Existing files keep their names until you edit their titles, and one switch in Settings turns the behaviour off. A folder you open in any other app should make sense on its own.
