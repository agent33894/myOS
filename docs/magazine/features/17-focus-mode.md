Interrupted people work faster, but they feel more stress, and the leftover attention from one task hurts the next. The behavioral research rates those findings as moderate, and draws a design rule from them: no interruptions from the app.

Focus mode is that rule made visible. Press ⌘. and the sidebar disappears, the properties and top bar fade away, and Linked from and Unlinked mentions step aside. What remains is the page and a quiet **Exit focus** button with its Esc hint.

The caret line stays vertically centred as you write, typewriter style, so your eyes stay in one place. Other paragraphs soften to a fraction of their strength while the one you are writing in stays whole. If you prefer the page even, switch off **Dim other paragraphs** in Settings.

What focus mode does not have is as deliberate as what it does. There is no timer, no session count, and no focus score. The research suggested a plain countdown could at most be opt-in; 3.0 leaves it out. You leave when you are done, with Esc or the same keystroke.
