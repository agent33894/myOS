The market research found one opening clearer than the rest: the calm, guided daily routine that Sunsama charges $16 a month for, and that nobody offers on plain files on Linux. Plan my day is myOS's answer.

It opens with the date and one line: "Pick what you'd like to do today. You can change it any time." On the left, candidates arrive in groups: Carried over, Due soon, Flagged, In progress, Anytime, and Next steps from your projects. On the right, **Today's plan** fills as you pick, with a running estimate against your available hours.

Drag to reorder, or focus a row and press ⌥↑ and ⌥↓. Picking a task writes `planned` into its file; the order you choose is kept too. **Set my day** ends the flow with "Your day is set", and Today lists the plan in your order.

Before three in the afternoon, Plan my day is the leading button on Today; after that, Close the day takes its place. Neither ever opens on its own. And if the plan is empty, the app says what the research would: "A day with nothing planned is a fine day too."
