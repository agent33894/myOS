Recalling something beats rereading it. The behavioral research rates retrieval practice as strong evidence, with effects around g = .50 in one meta-analysis and .61 to .70 in another, and spacing the attempts helps retention further. Most note apps are built for rereading. Recall is built for remembering.

On any note, choose **Review this note**. It comes back tomorrow, then at widening intervals: 1, 3, 7, 16, and 35 days if you keep answering Good, stretching or shrinking with each answer.

The Review page shows the title first and a single question: "What do you remember?" An optional box invites you to "Put it in your own words first, if you like. This stays here and isn't saved." That is the generation effect, which the research also rates as strong. **Show note** reveals the body, and you answer **Again**, **Hard**, **Good**, or **Easy**, each labelled with when the note will return.

The queue holds at most ten notes a day, and the rest roll forward quietly. There is no pile-up, no penalty for skipping, and the schedule lives in two readable frontmatter fields, `review` and `reviewInterval`.
