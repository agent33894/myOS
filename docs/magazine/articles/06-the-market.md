# The Beautiful and the Open

*Survey the notes and tasks apps of 2025 and 2026 and a pattern emerges. The calm ones live on Apple devices and keep your words in their own formats. The open ones keep your files and hand you a toolkit. myOS is betting on the space between.*

By the myOS Editors

---

Every season has its houses. In notes and tasks, they are familiar names, and for 3.0 the myOS research team read them the way a fashion editor reads the collections: what each is praised for, what its admirers complain about, what it costs, and, most importantly for this magazine, where it keeps your words.

What follows draws only on that market research, dated September 2026. Figures are as reported by the sources the research cites. Some of them, notably certain prices, come from secondary review sites, and the research itself flags those for spot-checking.

## The calm houses

The most admired apps in this category share a temperament. **Things 3** runs a clean loop from Inbox to Today to Upcoming, and users praise it for being calm and a one-time purchase; the research cites a review from AsianEfficiency listing its complaints as Apple-only, no collaboration, and slow development, with a total of about $80 across devices. **Bear** is loved for its beautiful editor and nested tags, and, per Toolradar, keeps notes in its own SQLite database rather than as files, with paid sync. **Agenda** places notes on a timeline, and is Apple-only. **iA Writer** and **Ulysses** are prized for typography and focus, and are not task or project tools. **NotePlan** comes closest to a whole system on plain Markdown, joining daily notes, calendar, and tasks, and its reviewers note that it gets complex and is Apple-only.

The research compresses this into one line: the calm apps, Things, Bear, Agenda, NotePlan, iA Writer, are Apple-only and mostly lock data in their own formats.

> The calm apps are Apple-only and mostly lock data in their own formats. The open apps are free but complicated.

Some calm houses have changed direction. According to the sources cited, **Craft**, once praised as the "prettiest notes app", is now built around AI and MCP on a subscription, and **Amie** has pivoted toward an AI notetaker. **Sunsama**, whose whole reason for being, per ClickUp, is its guided morning plan and evening shutdown, costs $16 a month with no free tier, and lives in the cloud.

## The open houses

On the other side of the street are the apps built on open files. **Obsidian**, in the research's words, is the largest local-Markdown community. Users praise it for owning their files, its plugins, and Bases, its built-in database views. A Substack essay the research cites names the complaint: a "plugin rabbit hole", more tweaking than writing.

The plugin charts, as tallied by obsidianstats as cumulative downloads, are a map of what people need and Obsidian does not ship: Excalidraw at 8.2 million, Templater at 5.7 million, Dataview at 5.0 million, Tasks at 4.3 million, Git at 3.2 million, Calendar at 3.1 million, Kanban at 2.7 million, Remotely Save at 2.3 million.

**Logseq**, the daily-journal outliner, has long been built on Markdown files. Per the Hacker News and forum discussions the research cites, its database version, in beta as of July 2026, makes the database, not Markdown, the source of truth. **Anytype** keeps data locally in an encrypted database; Toolradar's review notes that its types and relations confuse people and that adding a tag takes many clicks. **Tana** is praised for structure, and, per a review on Saner, takes weeks to learn and is cloud-only.

## The cloud houses

Then there is the cloud. **Notion** is flexible; the sources the research cites describe it as slow, its offline mode as feeling "fake", and its databases as exporting to CSV, which amounts to lock-in. **Todoist** has what reviewers call the best date parsing in the business, the research cites 2sync on this, with a reported price rise of about 40 percent in December 2025. **TickTick** puts tasks, calendar, habits, and Pomodoro in one inexpensive app, with weaker parsing of repeat rules. **Apple Notes** is free and everywhere on Apple devices, and, per MacRumors, gained Markdown import and export only with iOS 26.

## The gap

Lay the houses side by side and a space appears that nobody occupies. The research states it plainly: no one offers calm, plain Markdown files, a strong task model, and Linux support together.

Linux is the sharpest edge of it. The research cites a usevoicy roundup finding Linux users left with Joplin or a heavily configured Obsidian. Things and Sunsama, the apps whose calm daily routines users praise, are Apple-only or cloud-only.

> No one offers calm, plain Markdown files, a strong task model, and Linux support together.

myOS already stands in that space. It is a local-first app for macOS and Linux, including Omarchy and Hyprland. It keeps plain Markdown in a folder you choose. It has no account, no sync service, no telemetry, and no bundled AI. And since 2.0 it writes untouched blocks back byte for byte, which the research calls a technical moat. Its proposed line for this: "Your files will still open in 2046."

## Five openings

The research names several differentiating opportunities. Five of them shape 3.0.

**A refuge from lock-in.** With Logseq moving its source of truth to a database, Bear and Anytype on their own databases, and Notion's exports described as lossy, a folder of plain Markdown is a quiet form of insurance.

**Calm planning on plain files, on Linux.** A Things- or Sunsama-style daily routine, running on your own files, on Linux, where the calm houses do not go. 3.0's *Plan my day*, *Close the day*, and *Weekly review* answer this directly.

**Obsidian without the plugin tax.** Ship the behaviours of the most-downloaded plugins as a small set of built-in, opinionated defaults. 3.0 brings repeating tasks, a journal, templates, and version history into the app itself.

**Friendly to Obsidian.** Read and write the same conventions, so one folder works in both apps. In 3.0, a checklist line dated with the Obsidian Tasks `📅` syntax appears in Today.

**Privacy that is literal.** No account and no network, rather than a policy asking you to trust someone's encryption.

## The collection that fits the gap

Fashion has a word for the piece that makes a wardrobe work: the one that goes with everything and draws no attention to itself. That, we would argue, is the role waiting for a notes and tasks app. Not the most powerful, not the most beautiful on one platform, but the calm one that keeps your files open and your desk clear on whatever machine you use.

That is the brief for 3.0.
