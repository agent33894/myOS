# A Folder, Opened as It Is

**This issue is about one app and one decision behind it: your notes and tasks belong in files you can read without us.**

By the myOS Editors

Most people who work at a keyboard already have a folder. It might be a `docs/` directory inside a repository, a folder of meeting notes that has moved between three laptops, or an Obsidian vault that has grown for years. The files in it are Markdown. They open in any editor. Git can track them. Nothing about them depends on a company staying in business.

myOS Next is an editor for that folder. It opens the folder as it is. It shows the real folders and files in the sidebar. It treats any `.md` file as a note and any checkbox line as a task. It does not ask you to move anything, rename anything, or add fields to your files. It adds nothing to a file that you did not type or ask for. When you check off a task, it writes the date, on that one line, and nowhere else.

> The app is a way of looking at the folder. The folder is still the thing.

That sentence is the subject of this issue. The cover story explains why a folder of plain files is a good home for a developer's notes and tasks, and what the app deliberately leaves out. Then we go page by page: how a single line becomes a task, how a one-line query becomes a view, why the daily note is where captured thoughts land, how saves are checked so that nothing is overwritten by accident, and how Git fits in without taking over. There is a tour of the keyboard, a look at the design, and a page on the `myos-next` command for people who would rather not leave the terminal.

We have tried to write the way the app is meant to work: say what a thing does, in plain words, and stop. Where we describe a shortcut or a line the app writes, we checked it against the source code. Where we quote the app, the words are the app's.

Each article carries a boxed reference: a syntax table, a list of keys, a sample of output. Tear them out if you like.

> Every example in this issue is a line of text you could type into any editor.

Thank you for reading.

*The myOS Editors*
